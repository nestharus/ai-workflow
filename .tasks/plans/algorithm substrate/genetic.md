### 1. The Trap: Why Single-Track Optimization Fails

If you have a single model that optimizes itself:

1. **Optimization:** It climbs the hill of efficiency (e.g., refactors code to be faster).
2. **Ossification:** As it gets more optimized, the code usually becomes more rigid and coupled (overfitting to the specific constraints).
3. **Local Optimum:** It reaches a point where any small change makes performance *worse*, but a massive change is too risky to attempt. It gets stuck.

### 2. The Shovel: How Decomposition Breaks Local Optima

Your **Algorithm Decomposition** tool acts as a **Structural Perturbation Function** (Simulated Annealing).

* **The Mechanism:** When a component becomes "Divergent" or "Complex", the Decomposer **shatters** the local optimum.
* **The Escape:**
* Imagine `COM-A` is at a local peak of efficiency.
* The Decomposer splits `COM-A` into `COM-B` and `COM-C` based on capability clusters.
* *Immediately*, the global efficiency might drop (due to overhead/latency between B and C).
* *However*, `COM-B` and `COM-C` are now effectively **new search start points** on the optimization landscape.
* The **Enhancer** can now optimize `COM-B` towards a completely different peak that `COM-A` could never reach because it was constrained by `COM-C`'s requirements.



**Conclusion:** The Decomposer allows the system to trade **short-term efficiency** for **long-term plasticity**, preventing the "Ossification" that kills self-improving systems.
