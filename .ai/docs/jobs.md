The Crisis of Identity:

        Traditional interviews test for Heroes (speed, syntax recall, clever hacks) or Guards (gatekeeping, finding bugs in diffs).

        AI orchestration makes these roles obsolete because machines handle syntax, verification, and mechanics better than humans.

        The industry is hiring for the wrong traits, resulting in "Senior" engineers who are just expensive linters.

    The New Role: The Operator/Architect:

        Definition: An engineer who designs the Ontology (the system of constraints) rather than writing the Implementation.

        Core Skill: Intent Stewardship. Can they carry a messy human requirement into a formal system without corrupting it?

        Behavior: They don't try to "impress" the machine. They try to make the machine "tell the truth" by forcing ambiguity into the open.

    The "Unicorn" vs. The "System":

        You don't need to hire rare geniuses ("Unicorns") who naturally think in constraints.

        You need to hire engineers willing to use the Translator Tool (the Top Layer) to externalize their thinking.

        The tool democratizes architecture, allowing "mediocre" engineers to function as high-level Architects if they have Integrity.

    The Interview Pivot:

        Stop asking: "Reverse a linked list."

        Start asking: "Here is an ambiguous goal. Design the constraints that make failure observable."

        The Litmus Test: Does the candidate rush to code (Hero)? Or do they stop to define the receipt/gate (Operator)?

Who to Hire:

    Look for Epistemic Seriousness: People who are uncomfortable with "guessing."

    Look for Structural Thinking: People who solve problems by building a process/gate, not by working harder.

    Look for Honesty: People who admit what they don't know and use the tool to find out, rather than faking it.
